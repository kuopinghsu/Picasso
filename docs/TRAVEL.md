# Trips and Stories

How Picasso organizes travel photos into Trips and writes them up as Stories: the model, who owns what, how photos keep their place in a Trip when files move or leave the library, and how capture times become days. The data layer is in `src/trip.rs` and `src/db/trips.rs`; the tables are listed in [DATABASE.md](DATABASE.md).

This page records decisions. Each one is changed only on purpose, together with its tests.

## Scope

Trips, Days, Stories, preview, and HTML and Markdown export work entirely on this computer: they need no website, AI service, or account. Publishing a Story to a self-hosted WordPress site is optional and builds on the same Story and rendering; it never replaces export.

Booking, payments, social features, hosting, a bundled web server, background sync, and automatic publication are out of scope. No WordPress theme is required: export and publishing work with any theme. An optional one is in `wordpress/voyagelight` (see Rendering, export, and publishing).

The interface shows only what works. A table may exist before the page that uses it, but no button, menu item, or setting appears for something that doesn't work yet.

## Concepts and ownership

| Thing | Belongs to | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Album** | the library | Unchanged. Trips don't use or change Albums. |
| **Trip** | the library | Title, description, destination text, optional date range, a timezone, an optional cover, and a status (active or archived). The date range describes the Trip; it never hides photos outside it. |
| **Day** | one Trip | An ordered section of a Trip, with an optional calendar date, a title, and notes. Days may be empty, and several Days may share a date. |
| **Trip member** | one Trip | A photo or video in the Trip, with its order and, optionally, one Day of the same Trip. A photo can be in several Trips, but in at most one Day of each. Having no Day is valid. |
| **Story** | one Trip | A narrative: title, subtitle, author and date display, cover, template, and an ordered list of blocks. A blank Story has no blocks but still has its Trip. |
| **Story block** | one Story | Heading, subheading, paragraph, list, quote, photo, gallery, photo grid, separator, credits, or route map, with a stable id and a versioned payload. |
| **Story media** | one block | A photo used in a block, with its own caption and alt text. The same photo can be in several Stories, each with its own words. |
| **Track link** | one Trip, optionally one Day | A link to a GPS track already in the library. The track's points are not copied. |
| **Place** (planned) | one Trip, optionally one Day | A stop on the Trip: a name, an optional name in the local language, coordinates, and notes. It is travel context only; it never changes a photo's place in the catalog. Not stored yet. |
| **Resource** | one Trip | A document, link, note, or track file kept with the Trip for reference: its kind, reference, title, notes, and whether it may be published. Private unless the user allows it, one resource at a time. |

Rules that follow:

- Trips, Days, and Stories never change a photo's stars, caption, date, edits, place, Albums, or file. Their text is separate from catalog captions unless the user explicitly copies it over.
- New Story photos are picked from the Story's Trip. Adding a photo from outside the Trip adds it to the Trip as well, in the same action.
- Removing a photo from a Trip leaves the Story blocks that use it in place; they show it as removed from the Trip and can be repaired.
- Selection for a Story belongs to that Story. There is no shared "selected for story" flag on a Trip member.
- A Day of one Trip can never be given to a member of another Trip. The database enforces this, not only the interface.
- A suggested Day assignment and a user's assignment are stored differently. Suggesting again replaces only suggestions, never the user's Days, names, notes, order, or Story content.

### Types and versions

The types are in `src/trip.rs`:

| Concept | Type | Stored in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trip | `Trip`, `TripDraft` to make or edit one, `TripZone` for its timezone | `trips` |
| Day | `TripDay`; `DaySource` says whether a Day assignment was suggested or made by the user | `trip_days` |
| Trip member | `Member`, `DayAssignment` | `trip_media` |
| Media reference | the `media_id` of a `Member` or `MediaUse` | `media_refs` |
| Story | `Story` | `stories` |
| Story block | `StoryBlock` holding a `Block` | `story_blocks` |
| Story media | `MediaUse`: a media reference with its caption and alt text | `story_media` |
| Track link | `TripTrack` | `trip_tracks` |
| Export profile | `Profile` in `src/story_html.rs`: photo size and JPEG quality | each export's `manifest.json` |

Everything stored or written has a version, so a newer Picasso can change it without misreading what an older one wrote:

- **Blocks** are stored with a kind and a version. A block whose kind or version this build doesn't know is kept as it was read (`Block::Unknown`), shown as made with a newer Picasso, and can be moved or removed but not exported.
- **The export profile** has its own version, written to the manifest with the size and quality, and the manifest has a version of its own.
- **Trip packages** have a version in their header; a package newer than this build is refused with a message saying so.
- **The catalog** has a schema version, raised whenever a table is added or changed ([DATABASE.md](DATABASE.md)).

## Removing things

- **Archive** is the normal way to put a Trip away. An archived Trip keeps everything, including its Stories, and is found through an Archived filter.
- **Delete** a Trip only after a summary of what goes with it: its Days, members, Stories, and track links. Deleting never touches photo files, catalog data, or anything published on a website. Records of what was published stay as detached records, so a later publish can't post a duplicate.
- Deleting a Story removes its blocks; the photos stay in the Trip.
- Removing a photo from the library, or its file disappearing, never deletes Trip or Story content (see below).
- Library backups include Trips, Days, members, track links, and Stories, so restoring a backup brings them back with the photos.

## Media identity

A catalog row is not a durable identity: when its file leaves the library the row is deleted, and when the file comes back it gets a new row and id. Catalog ids are never reused (the `id_floor` table), but that only stops an old id from pointing at a different photo; it doesn't bring the old link back. Trips and Stories therefore refer to photos through **media references**:

- Each reference has its own id, which is never reused, and a link to the current catalog row that becomes empty when the row is deleted.
- It keeps repair hints: the path, the file name, the size, the modification time, and the date taken read from the file.
- When the link is empty, the reference is shown as unavailable. The Trip member and the Story block stay.
- After each scan, unlinked references are linked again: first to a photo at the same path, else to the one photo with the same size, modification time, and file date. If more than one photo matches, nothing is linked and the reference waits for the user to choose. A photo that already has a reference is never given a second one.
- References are kept as long as any Trip member, Story, cover, or publication uses them, however long the photo has been gone. The 90-day limit of the photo archive does not apply to them.
- References no longer used by anything are removed.

## Capture time and Days

The catalog's date taken is a display value. For a photo it is the camera's clock reading interpreted in the timezone the computer had when it was scanned, so on its own it does not say when the photo was taken in UTC, nor what the camera's offset was. Days are therefore worked out from separate provenance, and the catalog's dates are never changed for them.

Each Trip has an explicit timezone, chosen or confirmed when it is created: either a named zone (such as `Asia/Taipei`), using timezone data packaged with Picasso so it works offline, or a fixed offset (such as `+08:00`), shown as such and never adjusted for daylight saving. Days are calendar days in that timezone.

A member's instant is decided in this order:

1. An instant the user confirmed for this Trip member.
2. The file's clock reading with the offset the file records (`OffsetTimeOriginal`), plus any **Adjust date** shift the user made in the catalog. The shift is applied once, as the difference between the catalog date and the file's own date.
3. The clock reading without a recorded offset, read as Trip time once the user accepts, in the Trip's settings, that cameras without an offset were set to it. Until then the member stays without a suggested Day and is counted for review.
4. Videos, which record UTC.
5. No clock reading (the file's modification time stood in): **Needs review**.

**Suggest days** reads the clock reading and offset only from Trip members that lack them or whose file changed, on a background thread that counts the photos read beside the button and stops on **Cancel**, keeping what it read so the next run reads only the rest (`Database::read_captures`), never by rescanning the library, and never writes the files. It makes a Day for each date that has none, after the existing Days and in date order, and reports how many photos need review and why. The Days, the photos placed in them and the review reasons are written as one change, so a suggestion that fails partway leaves the Trip as it was. The time zone rules come from `chrono-tz`, compiled into Picasso. A clock reading that is ambiguous or doesn't exist in the Trip timezone (around a daylight saving change) goes to **Needs review**. With the same confirmed inputs, the same Days come out whatever timezone the computer is in.

A Trip with up to five Days shows them as chips; with more, one line holds a Day selector with previous and next buttons and a ⋯ menu to edit, move, or delete the Day shown, so a long trip doesn't push the photos down. The trip page's rows, Show, Days, Tracks, and Stories, start their chips at one edge, and Photos and Stories tabs above them switch the page between the trip's photos and its Stories. Above the photos, Stories take one line however many there are: the Story worked on last, New story, and a link to all of them. The Stories tab shows each Story as a card with its cover, or its first photo, and whether it is a draft, finished, or archived; the last card starts a new Story. The tab chosen is kept for each trip while Picasso is open. Edit trip… and a ⋯ menu with Export trip package… are at the right end of the tabs' row.

**Places** sit under the Days in a Trip's header (`trip_places`). **Suggest places** groups the Trip's members that have a position, from the camera's GPS or a pin, never changing either (`trip::cluster_places`, `Database::suggest_places`): in the order taken, a visit goes on while each photo is within the distance (300 m unless changed) of the visit so far and the gap (2 hours unless changed) of the one before, and visits within the distance of each other are one Place, so coming back to a spot doesn't make another. A member with neither takes where one of the Trip's linked tracks was when it was taken, found as Geotag finds it (`geotag::Timeline`: along the track between points at most 30 minutes apart, else the nearest point within 30 minutes), with its time read as Suggest days reads it (Suggest places first reads, on its own thread, the capture times not read yet), in the Trip's time zone with daylight saving, so a clock reading just after the change lands at the right hour. That position is used only to group it; it is never written as a pin, and the message after Suggest places says how many were placed by a track. Each Place is a chip with its count that shows its photos; its menu edits it, moves it earlier or later, or deletes it, its photos staying in the Trip. **Edit place** gives a name, a name in the local language or script, and notes, and shows where it is. **Look up name** in it fills the name, in the app's language, and the local name from OpenStreetMap Nominatim (`geocode::reverse`). It is off until **Look up place names online** is turned on in Settings › Map, which says what is sent; it runs only when pressed, sends nothing but the position rounded to three decimals (about 100 m), at most one request a second, and keeps each answer in the cache folder (`places/`), so the same spot is named again offline. The names are only filled in; nothing is saved until **Save**. Nothing is ever looked up on its own. A Place the user named or moved keeps its name, notes, order, and photos when Places are suggested again, and takes in new groups within the distance of it; the others are suggested anew, after it in the order first visited. A Place without a name is shown by its local name or its number. Places are not yet carried in a Trip package; publishing can show a Story's Places on its post's map (see Rendering, export, and publishing).

**Geotag photos…** on a Trip's Tracks row, shown once a linked track is available, opens the folder's Geotag dialog with the Trip's photos and its available tracks chosen (`src/app/geotagging.rs`, `src/geotag.rs`). The places come from the ticked tracks, read as one timeline in which each point keeps its track, or, with **Photos that have GPS**, from the photos among them that have GPS and the library's other photos with GPS taken within a day of them, each timed by the offset it records. **Check** runs the same matching without writing and lists each photo, with its time, place, track and how far apart; it also tries as the camera's offset every one in use, the whole hours from −12:00 to +14:00 and the zones off the hour such as +05:30 and +05:45, scores each by how many photos land and how close to a track point, and names the best one when it places at least two and a tenth more photos than the offset chosen, so chance doesn't name a zone. Nothing is named when every photo was read in the offset it records, as such times are exact, and when a typed offset differs from the one they all record, that one is named if it places more; between equal scores, the one nearer the offset chosen wins, else nearer the most common offset the photos record, else this computer's. **Use** takes it and checks again. Changing a choice drops the check. A write records which track gave each place in `.picasso-geotags.json`; geotagging a Trip clears only its own photos' earlier entries from those tracks.

**Resources** sit under the Tracks in a Trip's header (`trip_resources`): documents and track files on this computer, web links, and notes. **Add resource…** asks for the kind, a title, the file, address, or text, and notes; Picasso keeps where a file is, never a copy. A click opens a document, track file, or link with the system, and edits a note; a file that is no longer where it was is marked missing, and a click or **Locate…** in its menu asks where it is now. Its menu also edits or deletes it, leaving any file in place. Every resource is private: **May be published** must be ticked for that one resource before anything published may show its title, notes, and link, and files are never uploaded. No Story, export, post, or Trip package includes resources yet.

Members Suggest days couldn't place are kept in **Needs review** with the reason, shown among the Days. Putting them in a Day, or in no Day, settles them; Undo brings the review back. Within a Day, photos keep the order the user gives them, whatever the grid's sort. **Move to Day** and **Arrange** in the toolbar, and their shortcuts (Alt+1 … Alt+9 for a Day, Alt+0 for none, Alt+↑ / ↓ / Home / End within the Day shown, Ctrl+Z to undo a move; ⌥ and ⌘ on macOS), go through the same operation on the selection in its order; the shortcuts do nothing while a text field has the keys.

The Trip's photos can be narrowed to the starred ones, those without a location, those no longer in the library, those without a caption, and those used by one of the Trip's Stories or by any of them, together with a Day. The grid, its count, and actions on it use the same query, and missing GPS or a missing caption is never treated as an error or stops anything else; **Not in a day** and **Needs review** narrow to the members without a Day and those with an uncertain time, and the same toolbar actions assign or repair them. In the Library, `is:notrip` searches for photos in no Trip and `is:intrip` for those in one; that is worked out from the Trips, with no separate inbox or review list.

Search is the library's in every view, so typing in a Trip shows the matching photos of the whole library and the Trip's own filters wait until the search is cleared. The Trip words carry the same meanings into a search: `trip:` matches part of a Trip's title or destination, `day:` a Day's title, `place:` a Place's name or local name, `story:` a Story's title, and `is:review` the members whose time needs review, each in any Trip; they combine with the other words and dates, and `-` leaves out what they find. A word is one word, so `trip:spain` finds "Spain 2025". Each Trip keeps its filters while Picasso is open, so going elsewhere and back finds it as it was; opening a photo and closing the viewer keeps the selection, as long as the photo last shown was part of it, and scrolls the grid to that photo.

**Similar photos…** at the end of a Trip's Show row suggests groups of photos taken close together that look alike, such as bursts (`src/burst.rs`, `src/app/similar.rs`). Each photo's look is a 64-bit difference hash of its thumbnail (grey, 9 × 8, each bit whether a pixel is brighter than the next); it is worked out on a thread of its own, with a count and **Cancel**, and kept with the thumbnail in the cache's `thumbs.db`, so it goes when the thumbnail does and a second search reads only new photos. Videos and photos without a time are left out. In the order taken, a photo joins the group of the one before it when it was taken within the window (10 seconds unless changed) and differs from it in no more bits than the **Alike** setting allows (12 unless changed); the settings regroup at once, without looking again. Each group shows its first photos, its size, and when it starts; **Select** selects its photos in the grid, to compare, star, or add to a Story by hand. Nothing is ever deleted, moved, or starred by the search.

GPS tracks already in the library are linked to the Trip from its header, to the whole Trip or to one Day. The link keeps the track's path and name, not its points; while the file is gone it shows as unavailable and comes back when the library finds it.

## Story editor

A Story opens in place of its Trip's photos. A new Story is blank, made from the selected photos, or made once from the Trip's Days or some of them: a heading for each Day and its photos in the Day's order, without invented text and without videos, which are counted and reported. Later changes to the Days don't change the Story's blocks.

A Story is about the whole Trip or about some dates, first to last (`Story::dates`), chosen when it is made with For some dates… and changed under its byline, each end typed or picked in the calendar; both left empty make it about the whole Trip. A blank Story made on the Trip's own dates is about the whole Trip, so it follows them when they change; one begun with their photos keeps the dates, since the whole Trip's photos are only those in Days. The dates under its title are its own, or the Trip's (`trips::story_dates`). A member is on a date by its Day's date, or else by when it was taken in the Trip's zone, or else by the date the library shows (`Database::member_dates`), so a Story's dates pick photos without Days and before their times are read. The photo strip of Add photos… starts on those dates (`DayFilter::Dates`), in the order the photos were taken, and offers All days and each Day as well. Starting it with their photos gives each date with photos a heading, the title of the Day they are all in or else the date. A Trip package carries the dates.

A Story is a draft until the author clicks the **Draft** status beside the way back to the Trip, which then reads **Finished** and is clicked again to make it a draft; drafts are labelled in the Trip's list of Stories. Blocks can be edited, moved, duplicated, inserted, and removed, with undo and redo for the session. Photos are added from the Trip's grid with **Add to Story**, each as its own block, together as a gallery, or as a **photo grid**; each photo keeps its own caption and alt text in that Story. A grid has a layout of columns by rows (2 × 1, 1 × 2, 3 × 1, 2 × 2, 3 × 2, 2 × 3, 3 × 3, chosen to fit the photos when made) and one description for all of them; more photos than places make more rows, so none is left out. Each grid has its own layout and description, so one Story can mix them. A grid is also added in the editor, from **Add** at the end or **Add below** in a block's ⋯ menu, by choosing its layout; its empty places, and **Add photos…** on any gallery or grid, open a strip of the Trip's photos, all of them or one Day's, where a click puts a photo in the block or takes it out. Photos are dragged to a new place in their gallery or grid, or into another one in the Story, and from the strip into a place; a single photo can be dragged too, and dropping it on another single photo makes a grid of the two side by side; the bar where they will land shows before or after the photo under the pointer. The strip can show only **Starred only** photos, reading the stars and never changing them, and only those **Not in the story yet**, the block's own photos staying so they can be taken out; a photo already used elsewhere in the Story has a quiet dot. Undo takes a move back. Every photo, gallery, and grid block has a **layout** menu in its header: a single photo, a gallery, or a grid of a size; a choice the photos don't fit can't be picked. On a change of layout the photos' own captions stay, unseen in the grid, and the grid's description becomes a paragraph below the gallery. The page lays a grid out in its columns with the photos cropped to the same shape; Markdown, which has no grid, lists the photos and then the description. An older Picasso keeps a grid as a block from a newer version. The cover is picked from the Story's photos: while there is none, **Add cover…** in its place above the title shows them in a strip, and a click on one makes it the cover; a Story without photos says to add some first. The Story editor has no right-click menus: each action sits on what it acts on, on a dark bar readable on any photo, shown while the pointer or the keyboard is on the photo or the bar and hidden while anything is dragged. A photo in a block has **Use as cover** on its top-right corner, a single word on a small tile, and in a gallery or grid **✕** to take it out; the photo that is the cover carries a small **Cover** label instead of the button. The cover has its tools in its bottom-right corner: the frame's shape when it is framed, **Frame cover…** and **Remove cover**. A block's own tools float above it, grouped by thin dividers: what the block is; how it looks (layout, and text styles on the block being written in); where it is (↑ ↓); and ⋯. **Frame cover…** chooses the part of it the Story shows: a shape (the whole photo; XPan 65:24, Cinema 2.39:1, 2:1, 16:9, 3:2, 4:3, 5:4; 1:1; 4:5, 3:4, 2:3, 9:16; or Free, whose corners and edges are dragged to any shape, no thinner than 5% of the photo), a size, and a frame dragged over the photo, with Reset, Cancel and Done (`trip::CoverCrop`, `stories.cover_crop`). It is the Story's own: the file, the catalog edit, the photo where it appears in the Story's blocks and other Stories are unchanged. Preview, export and WordPress render the cover twice when it is framed, once whole for the blocks and once cropped for the cover or featured image (`Story::cover_key`, `story_html::crop_cover`); a new cover starts whole.

Below the Story's title and byline is an optional **Introduction**, and after its blocks an optional **Conclusion**: free text that opens and closes the Story. Left empty, the export leaves them out; written, the page shows the introduction before the first block and the conclusion after the last.

The editor works like a block editor: each block is drawn as it will read on the page, the width of the page, with photos at their size and headings and paragraphs in their type, and no frame until it is pointed at or clicked. Its tools then float above it: a handle to drag the block to another gap, its kind and photo layout, ↑ and ↓, and ⋯ for Duplicate, Add below, and Remove. A clicked block keeps its frame and tools, and a photo clicked in it or the cover keeps its bar, until a click elsewhere on the page or Escape, which lets go of the block and the keys; a click on its tools or on a menu or list they open doesn't. Escape closes the innermost thing open first (`StoryEditor::escape`). In order, that is the "/" menu (the words and caret stay, and it stays shut until the paragraph is typed in), the cover's framing (as Cancel), the cover's choices, and the photo strip (as Done, so an empty gallery goes). Only then does Escape let go of the block. The tools of a block scrolled out of view aren't shown, and the Story's keys (undo, redo, moving and duplicating) do nothing while a dialog or menu is open over it. Pointing at the gap between two blocks shows a line with **+**, which adds a block there; the end of the Story takes a click to start a new paragraph. An empty Story offers **Start writing**, **Add photos**, which adds a gallery with the strip of the Trip's photos open, and **Add a route map**; a gallery still empty when its strip is closed is taken away. Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+↑ and ↓ move the chosen block and Cmd/Ctrl+D duplicates it; a block is chosen by a click or by the keys arriving in it, and the tooltips of ↑, ↓ and Duplicate name the keys. In a heading or paragraph, Enter starts a new paragraph from the caret on, Shift+Enter breaks the line, and Backspace in an empty block removes it and goes back to the block before. Typing **/** in an empty paragraph lists the blocks by name, such as `/grid 3x2` or `/heading`, in a card under the paragraph with each block's mark and what it is for; ↑ and ↓ choose one, and Enter or a click makes the paragraph that block. Backspace at the start of a paragraph joins it to the end of the text block before, and ↑ at the start or ↓ at the end of a text block moves the caret to the text block before or after, so the Story types like one page. The text and photos are a column the width of a page in the middle of the window. At its top are the cover, across the page, the title and subtitle in large type, and the byline: the author, and the Trip's dates when shown; the blocks' text lines up with them. The bar above the page has the way back to the Trip, the Draft or Finished status, **Saving…** or **Saved** (a save writes the details, the cover and the blocks as one change, so one that fails, as on a full disk, keeps nothing and **Try again** saves it as it was; `Database::save_story`), and the word count; at its end, in groups, ↶ and ↷ (their tooltips name Cmd/Ctrl+Z and Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+Z); ⚙ for the Story's settings (its template, and whether the Trip's dates show) and ⋯ for Archive or Restore and Delete; then **Preview** and, last, a filled **Publish ▾** with **Export…** and **Publish to WordPress…**. Every toolbar of the editor, this bar, a block's tools, the bars on photos and the cover, the cover's framing and the photo strip's filters, has one design (`tool_bar`, `tool_button`, `tool_icon`, `tool_divider` in `story.rs`): buttons 28 points tall, square when they show a symbol or an icon, with no fill or outline until pointed at; rounded bars with a hairline and a soft shadow on the page, or dark and translucent on a photo, the only difference being colour; thin dividers between groups; a filled accent button for the one main action; and chips, tinted when on, for filters. The page itself follows the same rules. Blocks are offered in one kind of row (`block_row`): a tile with the block's mark, its name, and what it is for. The "/" card, the **+** menu, ⋯ › **Add below** and the empty Story's beginnings all use it; the **+** menu shows what each is for on hover, with the Photo grid's layouts beside it. An empty place to fill is drawn one way (`empty_slot`): a hairline outline with muted text that lights up when pointed at or reached by Tab. That covers **Add cover…**, a grid's empty places, and a Route map with no track yet, which is low until one is chosen. Buttons inside blocks, such as **+ Add photos…**, **+ Add a line**, a Practical info line's ✕ and **From the trip…**, are borderless like a toolbar's, with "+" actions in the accent colour (`page_button`). A Route map's track list sits in a toolbar under the map. A chosen block's frame, a Practical info box, empty places and menus share one corner radius. Each gap between blocks is named "Add a block here" for screen readers and shows its **+** when Tab reaches it. The end-of-page prompt to start writing is hidden while the Story is empty. The Introduction and Conclusion are named only once they have words or the caret, and their room is kept so the page doesn't jump. A photo's empty caption and its alt text show only on the block that is chosen, so the page reads as it will look, and the bar above it counts the words and the minutes they take to read, counting each Chinese, Japanese, or Korean character as a word.

Text blocks are a heading (H2), a subheading (H3), a paragraph, a list, bulleted or numbered, a quote with who said it, and credits. Typing `# ` at the start of a paragraph makes it a heading, `## ` a subheading, `- ` or `* ` a list, `1. ` a numbered list, `> ` a quote, and `---` a separator with a new paragraph after it, where writing goes on. In a list, Enter starts a new item from the caret on, Enter on an empty item ends the list there with a paragraph, the items after it going on as a list below it, and Backspace on an empty item removes it; the only item turns the list back into a paragraph. The tools of the text block being written in also have B, I, S, `</>`, and 🔗, which put bold, italic, strikethrough, code, or a link around the selection, as do ⌘B, ⌘I, ⌘⇧X, ⌘E, and ⌘K, and pressed again take it away; the A with a colored bar under it opens six text colors, red, orange, green, blue, purple, and gray, and No color, which takes the selection's color away; H2 and H3 switch a heading's level, and • and 1. the kind of list.

A **Practical info** block holds facts to plan with under a title, as label and value lines such as "Getting there" and "Train from Lisbon, 2 h"; lines are added and removed in the block. The page shows it as a boxed list of terms, Markdown as a bold title and a list, and WordPress as a `core/group` (class `picasso-info`) with a heading and a list. A **Link** block is a web page to read more: its title, address, and a note. **From the trip…** in it fills it from one of the Trip's link resources, offering only those allowed to be published. Only an `http` or `https` address is ever made a link (`trip::web_address`); anything else is shown as its title in plain text, in the page, Markdown, and WordPress (`core/paragraph`, class `picasso-link`). Lines and blocks left empty are left out.

A **YouTube video** block (kind `youtube`) is a video's address and a caption. The address can be any of YouTube's own: a `youtube.com` watch, shorts, live, or embed page, or `youtu.be`, with its start from `t` or `start` (`trip::youtube`); a playlist, or anything else, is marked under the field and never linked. While writing, the block shows a card in the player's place with the video's id and start, which opens the video on YouTube; nothing is fetched from YouTube in Picasso. The page shows the same card, drawn by its styles, as a link that plays the video on YouTube, with the caption below. YouTube's own player is left out: the page then needs nothing from the network, and the player won't play in a page opened from its folder, as the preview is. Markdown makes it a link to the video, and WordPress a `core/embed` block of the video's address, which WordPress turns into YouTube's player; that player starts at the beginning, so the VoyageLight theme and the Picasso Places plugin start it where the address says (`embed_oembed_html`). A block with an address that isn't a YouTube video's keeps only its caption, and export and publishing say how many there are.

A **Video** block (kind `video`) is one of the Trip's videos with a caption and alt text, like a photo, and the part of it the Story shows, `start` to `end` in milliseconds, an `end` of 0 meaning the video's end (`trip::clip_range`); a part shorter than half a second (`trip::CLIP_MIN_MS`), or ending before it starts, is read as an unknown block. Adding a Video from the + or "/" menus opens a chooser of the Trip's videos on the Story's dates, or all of them when none is on those dates; **Replace video…** in the block's tools opens it again and keeps the caption and alt text. **Add to Story** puts the selected videos after the photos, each as its own block, and a Story made from the Days still leaves them out. The block shows the part's first frame with its length, or "1:00 of the video's 3:00" when it is a part, and plays in place with sound through the viewer's player (`video::Player`), its frames decoded no larger than the block shows them, stopping at the part's end and when the block is scrolled out of view or the Story closed. Under it, a timeline lights the part between two handles, dragged or moved by ← and → (a tenth of a second, a second with Shift) once Tab reaches them, each a slider for screen readers, with the time over a handle while it is dragged or has the keys; moves of one handle in a row are one undo step. A drag along the line moves a playhead over the whole video, and a click moves it there, kept within the part while it plays. Each moment dragged to is shown as its exact frame by `video::Scrubber`, which has FFmpeg decode single frames on its own thread and skips those asked for while one is decoded, so the frame keeps up with the drag however slow the file is to seek; playing goes on from the frame shown. Two buttons beside the times start or end the part at the frame shown, when that leaves at least half a second. Export and publishing make only that part, with FFmpeg, into an MP4 every browser plays (`video::web_video`): H.264 within 1920 × 1080 either way up and 60 frames a second, from libx264 or else the system's encoder, AAC stereo sound, its index first so it plays while it loads, and none of the original's metadata, so never where it was taken. Parts are kept in the cache's `story-videos` folder by what they are made from (`story_html::clip_name`: the file's path, size and modification time, and the part), up to 4 GB with the least recently used removed first, so exporting again or publishing doesn't make them again. The page puts the part in `videos/` and a poster, its first frame rendered like a photo, in `images/`, and shows them as a `<video>` with the browser's own controls; Markdown links the poster to the part; `manifest.json` lists the parts under `videos`. Without FFmpeg, export says how many videos need it and can go on with "Video unavailable" in their place; a video whose file is gone counts as an unavailable photo does. Making a part shows its percentage and can be cancelled. WordPress gets the poster uploaded like a photo, then the part streamed from its file as it is read, never held whole, with no limit on how long the upload takes (`wordpress::Client::upload_file`), and a `core/video` block with them and the caption; the core block has no place for alt text, which goes to the upload in the media library. An upload of a part is known by what it was made from, so one on the site is reused without making the part again. Publishing needs FFmpeg for videos, and a site that refuses a part as too large, by status 413, its or PHP's own words, or finding no data where PHP dropped a body over `post_max_size`, stops the job with the part's size and the suggestion of a shorter part or a higher limit. A video is never the featured image.

A **Table** block is text in rows and columns: its rows of cells, every row as long as the others, whether the first is the header row, and a caption. It has at most 8 columns and 30 rows (`trip::table_fits`); one stored otherwise is kept as an unknown block. It is added at a size chosen on a grid of squares beside **Table** in the + menu, or by "/table 4x5" ("/table" alone makes 3 × 3), and its tools resize it on the same grid, turn the header row on and off, and add or remove a row or column at the cell that last had the caret, as a cell's right-click menu does. The grid shows a row more as the pointer reaches its last, the arrow keys and Enter choose on it too, and for a table its cells with text are marked: resizing never takes them away, only a cell's menu does. After a row or column is removed, or the table resized, the caret goes to the nearest cell. Enter goes to the cell below, adding a row after the last; ↑ and ↓ go between rows and on to the blocks around; Shift+Enter breaks the line, and cells take the same marks and format buttons as paragraphs. Rows and columns with nothing in them are left out of what is exported or published, and a blank header row is none.

Its columns fit their text until the lines between them are dragged, or moved 5 percent at a time with ← and → once Tab reaches them in the chosen table (or by a screen reader's increment and decrement, as a slider); then the block keeps `widths`, each column's share of the width in percent, at least 5 (`trip::TABLE_WIDTH_MIN`) and 100 together (`trip::table_widths_fit`). Widths that don't fit the columns are read as none. Adding, removing or resizing columns shares the width out again (`trip::spread_widths`), a table of one column has none, and double-clicking a line, or **Fit columns to their text** in a cell's menu, forgets them. A drag is shown as it goes and becomes one edit when let go (`TableEdit::Widths`), so each drag, key press and reset is one undo step. The editor shows columns without widths as equal, so the first drag also fixes the other columns at the shares shown.

The page draws a table as `figure.table`, styled by each template, with a `<colgroup>` of its widths, shared out again over the columns kept; Markdown as a table, with an empty header row when it has none, and no widths; and WordPress as `core/table` with `hasFixedLayout` off, so its columns fit their text, which the VoyageLight theme styles as `.wp-block-table`. A core table has no markup for widths, so they go in a `picassoColumnWidths` attribute, which the VoyageLight theme and the Picasso Places plugin draw as a `<colgroup>` (`render_block_core/table`). Editing the post in the block editor drops the attribute, since `core/table` doesn't know it.

Before export or publishing, the check also says how many photos are smaller than 1200 pixels on their long side (`LOW_RES_EDGE`, before any crop), which may look soft across the page, and, for export, that the Story has no cover. Both are only warnings; neither stops anything.

A **Route map** block draws one of the Trip's linked GPS tracks, or all of them, on the map: the closest zoom that shows all of it, from the map source chosen in Settings, with the line from a filled dot where it starts to a ring where it ends, its length in the corner, the map's credit and scale, and an optional description. The track is chosen in the block; its points, no more than 800, are copied into the block, so the Story keeps its route in exports, packages, and backups even if the track file goes. Choosing the track again takes its points anew. Export and preview draw the route on its map as a picture beside the photos, from the same tile cache, one zoom closer so it is sharp on fine screens, with the description, length, and map credit below; Markdown links that picture too. The picture has no place names, which OpenFreeMap draws separately. Without tiles, such as offline with nothing cached or a source missing its key, the page draws the route on its own in the page's colors. WordPress gets that drawing as a `core/html` block.

The styles are stored in the text as light Markdown marks, `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `~~struck~~`, `` `code` ``, a link as `[text]` with its address in parentheses after it, and a color as `{red}text{/red}`, drawn styled in the editor, with the marks dimmed while the block is being edited and hidden otherwise (`src/inline.rs`). A backslash keeps a mark a plain character, a mark without its closing half stays text, and only `http`, `https`, and `mailto` addresses become links. Text has one color at a time: a color opened inside another stays text, and choosing one for a selection takes away the color it had, the text around it keeping its own. Each color has a shade for light pages and a lighter one for dark pages, and the editor uses the one for the app's look. The page and WordPress get the styles as `strong`, `em`, `s`, `code`, and `a`, and the Markdown export keeps the marks. A color on the page is a `color-red` class, with the dark shade when the reader's system is dark and always on a Photo Essay; in a post it is Gutenberg's own inline color, a `mark` with the light shade and a `picasso-color-red` class, which the VoyageLight theme gives the dark shade in dark mode; Markdown, which has no color, gets it as an HTML `span` in the light shade.

Changes are saved after a pause in typing, and at most five seconds after the first unsaved one, when the Story is closed, and when Picasso quits. The editor shows whether the Story is saved. A save that fails keeps the changes open in the editor, with the reason and a way to try again; a Story changed elsewhere is never overwritten. A failed save isn't retried on its own, only by Try again or another edit. Closing the Story waits for a save that succeeds. Quitting saves first, and if that fails the quit is held back to ask: Try again, Keep editing, or Quit anyway, which leaves the library as last saved. Blocks from a newer version of Picasso are kept as they are, and missing photos are shown as unavailable.

## Rendering, export, and publishing

`src/story_html.rs` turns a Story into one web page: `index.html` with its styles inline and its photos in `images/`, named after a hash of the rendered bytes so unchanged photos keep their names. A Story's template, picked in its header, changes how the page looks, never its blocks, and the preview and export follow it (`trip::TEMPLATES`, `stories.template`; an unknown one reads as the first). **Photography Journal** has one column, large photos, system fonts, and light and dark colors. **Travel Diary** is a notebook page: warm paper, a narrower column, photos as bordered prints, italic headings. **Travel Guide** numbers the sections and lists them under **Contents** at the top, in the Markdown export too, with quotes as boxed tips and photos kept in the column. **Photo Essay** is a dark page with photos edge to edge, one per row, and the text in a narrow centered column. Every template keeps to system fonts and inline styles. Publishing to WordPress doesn't use the template; the site's theme styles the post. All text is escaped, and the page loads nothing from the network. Photos that can't be shown appear as unavailable rather than being left out; blocks from a newer Picasso are counted and reported.

**Export** writes the same page, or a Markdown file (`story.md`) with the same `images/` folder, into a new folder named after the Story inside the folder the user picks: `Title`, or `Title 2`, `Title 3` when that name is taken, so an earlier export or anything else there is never replaced. It is written under a hidden name first and renamed only when complete, so a failed export leaves no half-written folder. One cut short by a crash or a power cut leaves its hidden folder, which the next export of the Story into that folder removes; one another running Picasso is still writing is left alone (on Windows, where that can't be asked, one unchanged for a day is removed). The user picks the photo size (1200, 1600, or 2400 pixels on the long side) and JPEG quality; the last choices are remembered, with Reset to defaults. Photos never carry camera details or location. Before export, a check lists what needs attention: an empty Story, unavailable photos, and photos whose file can't be read (checked from the head of each file) block export until the user adds content or chooses to export those photos as unavailable; so does too little free space in the chosen folder for the estimated size of the export (checked on macOS and Linux; elsewhere a full disk fails the export, which leaves nothing behind); photos without alt text and blocks from a newer Picasso are warnings. Each issue links to the first block that has it, which closes the dialog and scrolls the editor there; exporting again checks anew. Export and preview show how many photos are rendered and can be cancelled, which leaves nothing behind. The finished folder opens when done. Beside the page, `manifest.json` records what it was made from: the Story and its revision, the format, the profile (its version, size, quality, and that no metadata is kept), and for each photo its media reference, the file it became, and the size and modification time of its source, without paths or names. A source file that changes while the page is written stops it, so a page never mixes two versions of a photo. An export runs on what the Story was when it started; edits made meanwhile go into the next one. After it, the Story's bar shows **Exported**, which opens the folder. The export keeps a key of what the page was made from (`story_html::content_key`): the Story's words, settings and cover frame, its blocks, the dates shown, and each photo's file, edit and rotation with the file's size and modification time, but not the revision, so saving without a change doesn't count. After each save or change to the library, the key is made again on another thread and compared. When it differs, the bar shows **Changed since the export**, which opens the Export dialog. Each comparison is numbered, and an answer to an older one is dropped. The last export of each Story is remembered until Picasso quits.

A route map whose tracks record height has a small elevation chart in its bottom-right corner, with the highest and lowest heights, in the Story editor as on the page and the post; the heights are read from the track files when the page is made, so a track without them, or whose heights are all 0 as some apps write when they recorded none, has no chart. In the editor they are read once the route is shown, on another thread, and the route is drawn without its chart until they arrive. A preview, export or publication started before then reads them on its own thread, so the page never leaves the chart out.

**Preview** writes this page to a folder of its own in Picasso's cache, replacing the previous preview of that Story, and opens it in the web browser.

`src/gutenberg.rs` writes a Story as WordPress block markup, the core blocks Gutenberg saves, for publishing to a site: a heading is `core/heading` and a subheading one of level 3, a list `core/list` of `core/list-item`s, a quote `core/quote` with its citation, each paragraph of a paragraph block a `core/paragraph` (credits with the class `picasso-credits`), a photo `core/image` with its caption and alt text, a gallery `core/gallery` of images, a grid a `core/gallery` of its columns with its description as the gallery's caption, a separator `core/separator`, and a route map its picture on the map tiles chosen in Settings (with its elevation chart, as in export), uploaded like a photo, as a `core/image` with its words, length, and the map's credit; when no tile can be had, its drawing in a `core/html` block. The introduction and conclusion are paragraphs before and after the blocks, and the cover is the featured image, or without a cover the Story's first photo. The site's media IDs and URLs are given when publishing, so the Story itself never holds anything from WordPress; a photo without an upload, or a block from a newer Picasso, which has no core form, stops it instead of being left out.


- A Story follows the catalog's current edits by default, and shows when its output would change because of them.
- Each export or publish job pins the Story revision, each photo's edit and rotation settings, the output profile, and the identity of each source file. It renders its own images before writing or sending anything, so edits made while it runs affect the next job. If a source file is missing or has changed, the job stops; it never substitutes other content.
- Output images are always rendered from the editing pipeline. Camera originals and RAW files are never copied or uploaded.
- Precise location and other EXIF data are removed by default; any location shown has to be approved first.
- Publishing keeps four things apart: the local content revision, the status last seen on the website, the sync state, and the state of the running job. A failed upload never erases the fact that an earlier version is published. **Save as new draft** makes a new explicit edition; retry and update reuse the existing edition and post.

`src/wordpress.rs` reaches a site's REST API. It takes the site's address as typed, so "example.com/blog" means `https://example.com/blog`, and finds the API from the page's `Link` header with the relation `https://api.w.org/`, then at `wp-json/` and at `?rest_route=/` under the site's folder, so a site in a subdirectory or without pretty permalinks is found too. A site that answers but refuses the API, as a security plugin can, is told apart from one that isn't WordPress. The site's title is shown with its HTML entities decoded. Checking an account reads `/wp/v2/users/me` with the account's Application Password and reports what it may actually do (upload, edit, publish); it never creates a post or an upload to test the connection.

- Only HTTPS. Plain HTTP, and private-network addresses typed as numbers, are allowed only for a site on this computer (`localhost` or a `.localhost` or `.test` name) in development. Addresses that only resolve to a private network are not yet checked.
- Redirects are followed by Picasso, not by the HTTP client, at most five times. A redirect to another origin stops the request, so the Application Password is only ever sent to the origin it was entered for.
- Each failure says what to do about it: no such site, certificate, timeout, not WordPress, API blocked, wrong user name or password, a site that ignores Application Passwords (WordPress takes them only over HTTPS or on a site whose environment type is `local`, and only once one has been made on the site; otherwise it answers as if signed out), not allowed, too large, too many requests (with the site's wait time when it gives one), or another status.
- The password is never written to logs or debug output.
- Photos are uploaded in one of two sizes (`src/upload.rs`): **Standard web**, the default, at most 2048 pixels on the longest side at JPEG quality 85, and **Low resolution**, at most 1280 pixels at quality 80. The quality is the JPEG encoder's setting, not a share of the original's quality. Each photo is rendered from the editing pipeline, with its edit, crop, and rotation applied before it is made to fit, and a smaller photo is never enlarged; RAW files go through the same decoder as the rest of Picasso. The camera original is never sent. With **Keep camera details**, on unless turned off and remembered with the size, the JPEG keeps only how the photo was taken, from its EXIF: the camera and lens make and model, focal length (and its 35 mm equivalent), aperture, exposure time, ISO, exposure compensation, flash, and the time taken with its offset, so a theme can show them beside the photo. It never carries the GPS position, serial numbers, owner or artist, maker notes, or XMP, whichever size is chosen; a photo without EXIF, or with the box unticked, is sent without any. Transparent pixels are laid on white, as JPEG has no transparency. Each rendered photo is known by the SHA-256 of its bytes, and its key names the size, quality, and background, so an upload of one size is never reused for another. Choosing a size never permits an upload by itself. The size estimate shown before rendering is replaced by the actual bytes before anything is sent.
- Sites are connected in Settings → Publishing: the site's address, the user name, an Application Password, and an optional name. Connect finds the API and checks the account on a thread, and only then keeps the password and adds the site, with what the account may do (upload, edit posts, publish, each shown with a mark as well as a color). Check signs in again with the kept password. Edit… changes the name or, with a new Application Password, rotates it; the note says to revoke the old one in WordPress. Disconnect, after asking, forgets the site and its password, and says how to revoke the password in WordPress, since forgetting it doesn't stop it working. A development site on this computer may use plain HTTP only when its box is ticked.
- **Publish to WordPress…**, in a Story's ⋯ menu, saves the Story as a draft on a connected site, and makes it public only when asked: **Publish it once saved**, offered when the account may publish and the post isn't already public, first opens a confirmation of what readers will see (the title, text, captions and photo descriptions; how many photos, and whether with camera details, never with where they were taken; and how many route maps show where the trip went), and the post is saved as published only after **Publish** there. **At a set time**, under it, takes a date and time (such as 2026-11-01 and 09:00) in the site's own time: the post is sent as scheduled with that local time, which WordPress turns into UTC by the time zone in its settings, summer time included, and the confirmation names when readers can see it. Before anything is sent, the job reads the site's clock (its offset from UTC in the API's index and the time its answer was sent) and stops, sending nothing, when the time is past or less than five minutes away, as WordPress would publish such a post at once. A site that publishes it at once anyway is said so. Picasso never takes a post down or makes a public one a draft again; that is done in WordPress. The dialog names the site and the account, the photo size, how many photos there are and about how large, and whether it changes the draft saved there before or makes a new one; sites whose account may not upload or edit posts can't be chosen. Each choice takes one row, with what it means in its tooltip. A **State** row says where the post stands, apart from its status on the site: up to date, changed here since the last save (the Story's revision differs from the one saved), edited on the site, or not confirmed, when a post was being made and the site never answered. Thumbnails of the first eight photos to be sent preview them under the photo size. **Categories and tags** names those chosen for this Story on that site; **Choose…** opens a window over the dialog that reads the site's own, every page of them, to tick or untick, with child categories under their parent and a search for long lists. A name typed in the search that the site doesn't have can be added there with **Add category** or **Add tag**, each a click of its own; it is made at the top level and chosen at once, and a name the site already has, as after a lost answer, is used rather than made twice. An account not allowed to make terms is told so. Chosen ones replace the post's categories or tags when it is saved; with none of a kind chosen, the post keeps its own, or a new post goes in the site's default category. The choice is kept per Story and site. An empty Story, unavailable or unreadable photos, and blocks from a newer Picasso block it; nothing is left out of a post. The main button is the approval: it names what it does, such as **Upload 7 photos and save the draft**, and the line above it says the photos go to the site first and can be reached by their address while the post is still a draft. The job (`src/publish.rs`) pins the Story as saved, first checks that an earlier post is still a draft (a deleted one is forgotten and a trashed one left alone, so the next save makes a new draft; one published or scheduled since is left alone, and the next time the dialog offers to update that post, keeping its status so readers see the changes at once, or to save a new draft beside it; one edited on the site since Picasso saved it, judged by a digest of its title, excerpt, content and featured image kept after each save (one kept by an earlier Picasso is replaced, not compared), is left alone too, and the next time the dialog asks, with nothing chosen, whether to replace those edits or save a new draft, and links to the post in WordPress to compare first. WordPress has no conditional write, so an edit made in the moments between that check and the save can still be replaced, as the Post row's tooltip says; a post's status is named in words: published, scheduled, pending review or private), renders every photo before sending any, three at a time, to a temporary folder removed when the job ends (one left by a crash or a power cut is removed by the next job), so the status line can give the actual bytes to send in place of the dialog's estimate (a photo rendered earlier in the session to bytes the site has, from the same file, unchanged in size and modification time, with the same edit, rotation, size, camera details choice and cover frame, isn't rendered again, as its bytes are known; it is rendered only if the site no longer has them, and the memory of what photos rendered to ends when Picasso quits), then reuses an upload of the same bytes that is still on the site, at the address the site gives it now (read a hundred at a time, so a large Story costs few requests), so a site that moved or changed its media folder gets the new address, and uploads the rest one at a time, each new upload given the photo's description in the Story so the site's media library offers it when the photo is used again (an upload already on the site is left as it is, as other posts may use it), and only then creates or updates the post, with the cover as its featured image and the subtitle as its excerpt, sent even when empty so a subtitle taken off leaves no stale excerpt. A read that times out, loses its connection or meets a server error is tried up to three times, after a second and then two, each with a little added at random; a request the site turns away for coming too often is tried again after the wait it asks for, up to 30 seconds, as it wasn't done. Cancel ends such a wait at once. A longer wait, a refusal of the account or of what was sent, and a write whose answer was lost are not tried again. When the site's answer to an upload or a new draft is lost, by a timeout, a dropped connection or a server error, Picasso doesn't send it again: it looks on the site for an upload of the same name, as the same bytes are always named the same (each upload is named for the Story's title and the photo's file name, in lowercase letters, digits and dashes and cut short, then part of a digest of its bytes, such as `spain-2025-07-01-img-1234-3fa2b1c9.jpg`, so the site's media library stays readable), or for a draft with the Story's title made since, and uses it; with no match the job stops and the next try sends it. When the library has no record of a Story's post on a site, as after restoring a library older than the first save, Picasso first looks there for a draft or published post with the Story's exact title; if one is found, nothing is sent, and the dialog asks whether to update that post, keeping its status, or make a new one beside it, with a link to open it in WordPress. A library restored to an older save of a post Picasso has changed since finds the site's version different from its record, so the edit check stops and asks rather than sending the older content. Before a new draft is made the intent is kept in the library, so after a crash or a restart a draft made then is found and changed instead of made twice; if more than one draft could be it, none is chosen and nothing more is made until the extra ones are deleted on the site. Each confirmed upload is recorded the moment it lands, so a cancelled or failed job is resumed without sending those photos again; photos that reached the site stay there. Saving deletes nothing on the site by itself. When a save of the same post, or of a Story's posts by Day with no single post beside them, leaves out uploads the posts used at the last save (`publication_media`, photos, videos and maps alike) that Picasso uploaded and no other Story's post on that site uses, a window opens once no other is open, listing each by its file name, ticked, with **Delete from site** and **Keep**; closing it keeps them all. It says the deletion is final, as WordPress keeps no trash for uploads, and that an earlier revision of the post restored in WordPress would show them missing. The job (`publish::delete_unused`), refused while that site has a publishing job running, first looks for each upload's file name, without the "-scaled" of a large photo, among the site's posts and pages in every state but the trash (published pages only, for an account that may not edit pages); one found there, or with a name too short to look for, is kept, as a post written in WordPress, an earlier post left beside a new draft or a Day post whose heading was removed may show it. The rest are deleted (`POST /wp/v2/media/{id}?force=true&_method=DELETE`, as a deletion WordPress takes in a POST; one already gone counts as deleted) and their records forgotten, so one used again is uploaded again. A save that made a new post, or switched a Story between one post and posts by Day, offers nothing, as the earlier posts stay on the site. When done, the post's edit page opens in the web browser. **Posts** offers, for a Story with Days (its top-level headings), **An index and one post per Day**: each heading starts a Day post titled "Story title – heading"; what comes before the first heading goes in the index post, with the introduction, the conclusion, the subtitle as its excerpt, the cover, and a list linking to each Day; each Day post ends with links to the Day before, the index and the Day after (as `?p=` links, which WordPress sends on to the post's address). The job (`publish::run_days`) first checks every post made before for the Story on that site, and stops before sending anything when one was edited on the site or is no longer a draft, unless **Change the posts edited on the site or already public** was ticked; then makes an empty draft for each part without a post, looked for by its title when the answer is lost, so every post has an id to link to; then saves each part as one post is saved, sharing uploads between them. Each post is recorded in the library the moment it is made or saved, keyed by its heading block's own id (`publications.output_key`, `index` or `day:` and the id), so renaming or moving a Day keeps its post and a stopped job resumes without making any twice. A Day whose heading is removed keeps its post on the site, untouched. The dialog starts with **By Day** as the Story last went to that site: ticked when its Day posts were saved after its one post. A folded **Summary** in the dialog lists, before anything is sent, what saving changes on the site: the post changed (its address unchanged) or made, with about the address WordPress will give a new one from its title (accents dropped, a number added by the site when it is taken); who can read it; how many photos, with how their uploads are named; route maps; the categories and tags, or that the site's own are kept or its default used; the excerpt; and the featured image. Every job is kept in the library's publishing log from the moment it starts, each confirmed upload counted as it lands, and how it ended is added at the end; the dialog's folded **History** lists the last five for the Story on that site, with when, how it ended and the photos sent. A Story is published to a site by one job at a time: a job keeps running when its Story is closed, and publishing the Story to that site again is refused until it ends. A preview, export or publishing job ends only itself when its result arrives (each result carries its job's own progress), so one left running when its Story was closed never ends one started since. A job that never ended, as when Picasso or the computer stopped, shows as **Stopped before it finished**, with the photos that reached the site, which the next job reuses. Both sizes follow the same rules: photos are rendered from their edits in sRGB, as the editor works, and sent as 8-bit JPEG without a colour profile, which browsers take as sRGB; location and serial numbers never go, and camera details only when kept. JPEG keeps no transparency, so transparent areas are laid on white, and the dialog says how many photos have them, read from the headers of PNG, WebP, GIF and TIFF files. Switching to Low resolution doesn't remove the larger photos uploaded before. WordPress doesn't tell Picasso its upload limit beforehand; a photo the site refuses as too large, whether by status 413 or by WordPress's or PHP's own words, stops the job with that said, suggesting Low resolution or a higher limit on the site, and Picasso never switches the size on its own.
- **Places** in the publishing dialog, shown when the Story's photos are in the Trip's Places, is off until **Show places on the post's map** is ticked; each Place can then be left out, and **About 1 km** rounds positions to two decimals (else six). What a site keeps is read from the fields its posts take, which `OPTIONS` on `/wp/v2/posts` lists to anyone (`wordpress::Client::travel_support`): the dialog reads it, without the password, when a site is chosen, and the job again before it sends anything. A site that lists `_vl_lat` and `_vl_lon`, as with the VoyageLight theme or the Picasso Places plugin, gets the Places as post meta for its own map (`publish::map_meta`): the first Place as `_vl_lat` and `_vl_lon`, and every Place, including one on its own, as a `_vl_stops` line, `Name | latitude | longitude`, in the order the Story first shows their photos. Turning it off sends empty values, which removes them from the post. Any other site gets them at the end of the post instead (`gutenberg::places`): a **Places** heading, a map picture with a numbered pin for each (`story_html::places_map`, drawn on the map tiles of the route maps, no closer than town level, uploaded as `…-places-…` and credited like them; left out when the tiles can't be had), and an ordered list of their names, each linked to it on OpenStreetMap; no fields are sent. A Day post takes the Places of its Day's photos and the index takes them all. A site that won't say is sent the fields, and when its reply leaves them out the job says the post shows none. The choice, the Places left out and the precision are kept per Story and site (`publication_places`), and the confirmation names the Places before a post goes public. **Link to the trip's Journey** makes a `vl_trip` post (a Journey in the VoyageLight theme) for the Trip as a draft the first time, kept per Trip and site (`publish_journeys`) and found again unless it was trashed, sets each post's `_vl_trip_id` to it, and, with places shown, rewrites its `_vl_trip_days` lines for these posts as `Day | Place | latitude | longitude | note | post ID`, keeping lines for other posts, lines whose post-id column is not one of these posts, and lines it can't read. The day is at least 1 and at most 9999, the furthest day the theme reads, so a later day is written as 9999 and still shown. The Journey is published with the post when the post is published now, and one that was already scheduled has its date set to the site's time, as the post does, because WordPress would otherwise keep the future date and leave it scheduled. It is scheduled for the same time when the post is, while it is still a draft or already scheduled. Journeys come with the VoyageLight theme or the Picasso Places plugin, which list `_vl_trip_id`; on a site without them the dialog explains so and can't link one, and a job sent anyway saves the posts without a Journey and says so. A Journey that can't be updated afterwards is reported without undoing the post.
- `src/keychain.rs` keeps each Application Password in the system's own credential store, filed under "Picasso WordPress": the macOS Keychain, Windows Credential Manager, or the Secret Service on Linux. The library holds only its reference, the user name and the site's origin, so backups, exports, and Trip packages never carry it. A password read from or saved to the store is also kept in memory until Picasso quits, so macOS asks to unlock it at most once a session. Where no store can be used, the password lasts until Picasso quits, and the person is told it won't be remembered; a store that refuses for another reason is shown as an error instead. The map keys in Settings are sealed into the settings file for this machine (`src/secret.rs`); that is not used for publishing passwords, which a copied settings file or backup must never carry.

An optional classic theme for a travel site, VoyageLight, is kept as source in `wordpress/voyagelight`; `make theme` checks its translations and writes the ZIPs that WordPress's **Upload Theme** takes to `wordpress/`, an English and a Traditional Chinese one, the same bytes each time. Its start page leads with a photo, the latest stories, Journeys, destinations and an atlas of posts that have a location. Its text is English or Traditional Chinese, by the ZIP chosen, whatever the site's **Site Language** (`languages/zh_TW.po`, built into a `.mo` in the Chinese ZIP). Its fonts are part of it, and a map loads Leaflet and OpenStreetMap tiles only after the reader presses **Load map**. Picasso's posts show in it like any other, with the camera details kept in a photo under it and the credits paragraph in smaller type; a post's places and its Journey come from Picasso only when **Places** and **Link to the trip's Journey** are ticked, and are otherwise entered in WordPress, as Destinations always are. Journeys, Destinations and Place types are its own, so they are hidden while another theme is active. For any other theme, the Picasso Places plugin in `wordpress/picasso-places`, built by `make theme` beside the theme, adds the same post fields, Journeys, editor boxes and maps, shown after a post's content; it does nothing while VoyageLight is active. [wordpress/README.md](../wordpress/README.md) says how to build, install and translate both.

## Trip packages

**Suggest trips…** on the Trips page groups the library's photos by when they were taken, read from the library without scanning again (`trip::suggest_trips`, `Database::trip_shots`): a new group starts wherever a set number of days pass with no photo (2 unless changed), and groups with fewer than a set number of photos (10 unless changed) are left out. **All dates**, the default, takes every photo; **Taken from** and **to** limit it to photos taken on or between those days (either may be left empty), and photos with no date are then left out. Photos already in a Trip are left out unless that box is unticked, and photos with no date are counted but in no group. Each group, the latest first, names its dates, how many photos, its folders and cameras (the most used first), how many have a location, and how many are already in a Trip. Times are as each camera recorded them, so a camera whose clock was off can split or join groups; the window says so. Under **Camera clocks**, each camera in the library, the most used first, can be moved by whole hours (up to three days either way) to make up for a clock that was off; this changes only the grouping in the window and the days New trip starts with, never the photos, and setting it back to 0 undoes it. A group with a moved camera names it and by how much. **Create trip…** opens the usual New trip dialog with the group's photos, first and last day (in the computer's time zone, as the library shows days) and main folder as its title, all to change there before anything is made. Before photos are added, by New trip or Add to trip, the dialog says what adding them does, with nothing written until the button is pressed (`Database::preview_add_to_trip`): how many photos and videos, the dates taken, how many are already in this Trip (left as they are), how many have a location, how many are also in another Trip, how many have no date and so wait to be placed by hand, and how many may be copies of one before them, sharing its byte count, pixel size and file date. Possible copies are only counted, never left out or removed; the duplicate search in the library is where they are compared and removed.

A Trip package (`.picassotrip`) is one file that carries a Trip to Picasso on another computer: the Trip with its Days, every photo in its Day, every Story with its blocks, and the GPS track files linked to it, each with its Day. Export it from the Trip header, or from a Trip's menu in the sidebar or on the Trips page; import it with Import trip… on the Trips page. While either runs, the status bar shows a progress bar with Cancel; a cancelled export writes nothing, and a cancelled import removes the folder it made and adds nothing. Unedited, unrotated JPEGs are copied byte for byte; other photos are rendered with their edits as JPEG. Unavailable photos stay unavailable in the package. The package records each carried file's SHA-256. Import adds a new Trip and first looks for each photo in the library: a file of the same size and hash is used as it is, and nothing is written for it. Tracks are looked for the same way. The other photos and tracks go into a new folder named after the Trip inside the chosen folder, and are linked once the library scans that folder. A track whose file can't be read when packing is left out. A package with tracks needs this version of Picasso; an older one says it was made by a newer Picasso. Edited photos travel rendered, so they match only a file rendered the same way. The package holds the media itself, so it is not a backup of the library; edits travel baked in, not as editable settings.

## Module boundaries

- `src/trip.rs`: Trip, Day, member, Story, and block types, and their validation.
- `src/story_html.rs`: a Story as a web page, shared by preview and export.
- `src/wordpress.rs`: a WordPress site's REST API, behind an interface the tests answer without a network.
- `src/upload.rs`: the two upload sizes and the JPEGs rendered for them.
- `src/elevation.rs`: a route's height along its length, read from its track files, and its chart on the route map.
- `src/publish.rs`: the job that uploads a Story's photos and saves its draft, with no interface of its own.
- `src/keychain.rs`: secrets in the system's credential store, with a session-only fallback.
- `src/db/trips.rs`: the tables, and every change to them, each in one transaction. The interface calls these operations with explicit ids and selection order; validation never lives in view code.
- The Trips page, Timeline, Story editor, and exporter live in their own modules under `src/app/` and reuse the library's selection, thumbnails, viewer, editor, and background workers.

## Desktop interface

- Colors, text styles, spacing, corner radii, selection, and hover come from `src/app/theme.rs` (for example `accent()`, `text()`, `muted()`, `select()`, `primary_button`, `danger_button`). Light, dark, and System themes, and the teal accent, work as everywhere else. A missing pattern is added to the theme, not drawn locally.
- Trips is a sidebar destination like Albums and Places. **All trips** shows every Trip as a card with its cover (or first photo), dates, destination, and counts of photos, Days, Stories, tracks, and unavailable photos; the header's search finds Trips by title, destination, or description, and archived Trips are shown on request. Tab moves between the cards and Enter or Space opens one. The toolbar, panels, shortcuts, and selection behave as in the Library.
- Each task has one primary action. Secondary actions are grouped, advanced settings are revealed progressively, and nothing required is shown only on hover.
- Every state is visible: default, hover, focus, selected, disabled, loading, error, and success, with keyboard focus and a cue that isn't only color.
- A button that shows only a symbol or an icon (◀ ▶, ↑ ↓, ⋯, ★, the calendar) is named for screen readers with `theme::named` or `theme::icon_button`, and shows the same name on hover. Anything drawn by hand that can be clicked, such as a sidebar row or a photo tile, gives its name with `widget_info`, and a drop-down list that shows only its choice is named with `theme::name_picker`. A control with no name of its own is named by its tooltip, so one with a long tooltip, like the search field, is named first. The headless test in `src/app/headless.rs` fails on any control without a name.
- An area that draws itself instead of being a widget, such as a Trip or Story card, a photo in a Story block, or a photo in the picker, gets a name with `widget_info`: a card its title, a photo its alt text or else "Photo 2", the picker's photos as checkboxes that say whether they are chosen. While the keyboard is on it, it shows the accent ring (`theme::focus_ring`), as the cards do.
- egui draws a focused button like a pressed one. While Tab moves the focus, `theme::keyboard_focus_ring` draws the accent ring around whichever widget has it; a click puts the ring away again. Actions at the right end of a header are added in reading order with `theme::trailing`, so Tab goes through them left to right; dialog button rows are still laid out from the right.
- Shortcuts wait only while a text field has the keyboard (`text_edit_focused`), not whenever something has focus. A control reached with Tab keeps Enter and Space, which click it, and the arrows, which move the focus; an area that uses the arrows itself, as a photo of the grid does, claims them with `set_focus_lock_filter` while focused and moves the focus along with what it chooses.
- Panels adapt to the smallest window (880 × 560) and to high DPI; long translations wrap rather than hide actions. A header with actions at its right end, such as the Library's sort and selection tools, the trip's tabs with Edit trip… and ⋯, or the Story's ↶ ↷, ⚙, ⋯, Preview, and Publish ▾, puts them in `theme::end_actions`, which remembers how wide they were and moves them to a line of their own when they don't fit beside what comes first. The top bar's search field leaves room for the actions after it by the width `theme::trailing_width` remembers, so they stay in the window at its smallest size.
- Desktop controls are native egui. Only the Story's browser preview and exported pages use web layout and typography of their own.
