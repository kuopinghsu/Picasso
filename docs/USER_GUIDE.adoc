Picasso is a photo library, viewer, and editor for your own computer, in the spirit of Picasa.
It watches the folders you give it, shows their photos and videos by date, and edits photos without changing the files.
Nothing is uploaded: the only things Picasso fetches from the internet are map tiles and, if you allow it, the number of the latest version.

This guide walks through Picasso as you use it.
To build Picasso from source, see the https://github.com/kuopinghsu/picasso/blob/main/README.md[README].

NOTE: Shortcuts are written with kbd:[Ctrl].
On a Mac, use kbd:[⌘] instead, so kbd:[Ctrl+A] is kbd:[⌘+A].

[#install]
== Installing Picasso

Download Picasso from the https://github.com/kuopinghsu/picasso/releases/latest[latest release].
Use `x64` packages on Intel and AMD processors, and `arm64` ones on 64-bit Arm processors.

Windows::
Download the `.exe` and run it; it needs no installing.
The `.zip` holds the same program with the READMEs and the license.

macOS::
Open the `.dmg` and drag Picasso to *Applications*.
Unless the release notes say the app is notarized, the first launch is blocked.
Click *Open Anyway* in *System Settings → Privacy & Security*, then confirm.
On macOS 14 and older, Control-clicking Picasso and choosing *Open* also works.

Linux::
The `.AppImage` runs on most distributions without installing:
+
[source,bash]
----
chmod +x picasso-<version>-linux-x64.AppImage
./picasso-<version>-linux-x64.AppImage
----
+
On Debian and Ubuntu, `sudo apt install ./picasso-<version>-linux-x64.deb` installs Picasso with its menu entry.
The README describes the `.tar.gz` and what to do where FUSE is missing.

[#ffmpeg]
=== Videos need FFmpeg

Picasso plays videos with https://ffmpeg.org[FFmpeg].
Without it, photos work and videos are skipped; they are added once FFmpeg is installed.

* Windows: `winget install Gyan.FFmpeg`
* macOS: `brew install ffmpeg`
* Linux: `sudo apt install ffmpeg`, or your distribution's package

If you can't install software, put `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` in the folder that holds Picasso.

== The first start

On its first start Picasso adds your Pictures folder and reads it in the background.
The status bar shows how far it has got, and photos appear by date as they are found.
You can use Picasso while it scans.

To add another folder, click *+ Import* at the top right, or drag the folder onto the window.
Picasso adds every subfolder too.

Picasso never moves, renames, or rewrites your photos.
Stars, captions, albums, places, dates you fix, and edits are kept in Picasso's own library, and only *Move to trash* changes a photo file.

== The library window

The window has four parts:

The sidebar::
*Library* shows every photo.
Below it are *Starred Photos*, *Places*, *Statistics*, *Travel Map*, and *Duplicates*, then your albums and the folder tree.
Each folder shows how many photos it holds.

The top bar and the toolbar::
The top bar holds the search field.
Above the grid are the title and photo count, the sort order, and buttons that act on the selected photos.

The grid::
Thumbnails grouped by the day they were taken.
When the photos are sorted by date, a strip beside the grid lists the years, with the months of the year in view; click one to jump there.
Dragging the scroll bar shows the date in view.

The status bar::
Scanning progress, the *Photo Tray* with the selected photos, and a link to files that couldn't be read, when there are any.

At the top right are *Slideshow*, *Refresh*, *+ Import*, the gear for *Settings*, and *?* for *Help & Shortcuts*.
Click the logo at the top left for *About Picasso*, which shows the version.

Press kbd:[I] to show or hide the Info and Map panel beside the grid.
It shows the selected photo's camera details and where it was taken.

== Finding photos

=== Sorting

The sort menu in the toolbar offers:

* *Sort by Date*, newest first, or *Sort by Date, oldest*
* *Sort by Name*
* *Sort by Name, edited*, which shows only the photos you have edited

=== Searching

Type in *Search photos* at the top of the window to find photos that match every word you type.
Picasso looks in the file name, the folder path, the caption, the camera, the lens, and album names.

A date finds the photos taken then:
`2019` finds that year, `2019-05` that month, and `2019-05-17` that day.
You can also write `2019/05` or `2019.05`.
Dates and words combine, so `2019-05 beach` finds the May 2019 photos that match "beach".

=== Selecting

* Click a photo to select it.
* kbd:[Ctrl]-click adds a photo to the selection or takes it out.
* kbd:[Shift]-click selects every photo between the last one you clicked and this one.
* kbd:[Ctrl+A] selects every photo in the grid.
* The arrow keys move the selection.
* The ★ button in the toolbar selects the starred photos in view.

The selected photos appear in the *Photo Tray* in the status bar.
*Clear* empties it.

== Organizing photos

Most actions are in three places: the toolbar above the grid, the *Photo Tray*, and the menu you get by right-clicking a thumbnail.
They act on every selected photo.

=== Stars

Press kbd:[S], or click the star in the toolbar, to star the selected photos; do it again to take the star off.
*Starred Photos* in the sidebar lists them all.

=== Captions

Open a photo, press kbd:[I] for the details panel, and type in the *Caption* field.
Search finds photos by their captions.

=== Albums

Albums group photos from any folders without moving the files.

* To make an album, select photos and choose *New album…* from *Add to Album* in the toolbar.
* To add photos to an album, select them and pick the album from *Add to Album*.
* In an album, *Remove from Album* takes the selected photos out.
The files are not touched.
* To delete an album, right-click it in the sidebar and choose *Delete album*.
The photos stay in the library.

=== Rotating

Press kbd:[R] to turn the selected photos clockwise, or kbd:[Shift+R] to turn them counterclockwise.
The rotation is kept in the library; the file is not rewritten.

=== Fixing the date taken

For a camera whose clock was wrong, or a scanned print, select the photos, right-click one, and choose *Adjust date…*.

* *Shift* moves every selected photo by a number of days, hours, and minutes.
* *Set to* sets the earliest photo to a date and time, and moves the others with it.
* *Use the date in the file* undoes your changes.

The new date is kept in the library and survives rescans; the files are not changed.

=== Moving photos to the trash

Press kbd:[Delete], or click *Delete* in the *Photo Tray*, to move the selected photos to the system trash.
Picasso asks first unless you turned that off in *Settings → General*.

On Windows and Linux, the status bar offers *Undo* for two minutes, which puts the files back where they were.
On a Mac, restore files from the Trash in the Finder.
Either way, a photo that comes back within 90 days gets its stars, caption, albums, and edits back.

== Viewing photos and videos

Double-click a photo, or select it and press kbd:[Enter], to open the viewer.

* kbd:[←] and kbd:[→] go to the previous and next photo; kbd:[Home] and kbd:[End] go to the first and last.
* Zoom with the mouse wheel, kbd:[Ctrl] and the wheel, a trackpad pinch, or kbd:[+] and kbd:[−].
Drag to pan.
* kbd:[0] fits the photo to the window, and kbd:[1] shows it at actual size, every pixel.
* The filmstrip along the bottom shows the photos around this one; click one to go there.
* kbd:[I] shows the details panel with the camera settings, a histogram, the *Caption*, *Show in folder*, and *Show on map*.
* kbd:[F11] switches to full screen, and kbd:[Esc] goes back to the library.

The viewer's toolbar also flips between the light and dark themes.

=== Slideshows

Click *Slideshow*, or press kbd:[Space] in the viewer, to play the photos from the selected one.
kbd:[Space] pauses and resumes, and kbd:[Esc] ends the slideshow.
*Settings → General* sets how long each photo shows.

=== Videos

Videos show in the grid with a play badge and their length.
In the viewer, kbd:[Space] plays and pauses, and kbd:[M] mutes.
Videos can't be edited.

== Editing photos

Open a photo and press kbd:[E], or click *Edit*, to open the editor in place of the details panel.
Editing never changes the file.
Each change is a step saved in the library, and the photo is drawn from the original plus those steps.
An edited photo has a small sliders badge on its thumbnail; click it to edit the photo again.

image::editor.png[The editor]

At the top of the editor are four tools, *Adjust*, *Crop*, *Transform*, and *Masks*.
Below them are undo and redo, a ··· menu with *Undo all*, *Redo all*, *Reset all*, *Copy*, and *Paste*, a live histogram, and the *Before*, *Side by side*, and *Split* buttons with the clipping button.

=== Adjust

*Adjust* holds the sliders, in sections:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Section | What it does

| Profile | A starting look under the sliders: Color, Neutral, Vivid, Landscape, Portrait, or Monochrome
| Presets | 25 built-in looks with an *Amount* slider; save your own settings as a preset to reuse them
| Transform | Turn, tilt, rotate by 90°, and flip without leaving *Adjust*
| Light | Exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, and blacks
| Tone curve | A curve with four zones, and point curves for RGB, red, green, and blue
| HDR | Opens shadows and brings back highlights in one photo
| Color | White balance, with an eyedropper for something neutral; vibrance and saturation
| Color mixer | Hue, saturation, and luminance for eight colors
| Color grading | Color wheels for shadows, midtones, highlights, and the whole photo
| Effects | Texture, clarity, dehaze, vignette, and grain
| Detail | Sharpening and noise reduction
| Black & White | Monochrome with an eight-color gray mix
| History | Every step, from the first
|===

Double-click a slider's name to set it back to zero.
For a quick start, *Auto tone* in *Light* balances exposure and contrast, and *Auto* in *Color* sets the white balance.

=== Crop

Press kbd:[C] for *Crop*.
Drag the edges or corners of the frame, or drag inside it to move it.
Pick an aspect ratio, or *Free*; ⇄ swaps portrait and landscape.
The *Straighten* slider turns the photo and keeps the crop inside it.
Press kbd:[Enter], kbd:[Esc], or *Done* when you finish.

=== Transform

Press kbd:[T] for *Transform*, which puts a handle on each corner and on the middle of each side.

* Drag a corner to move just that corner, for example to make a leaning building stand straight.
* Drag the middle of a side to skew or stretch the photo.
* Drag inside the outline to move the photo, or outside it to turn it.

The thin frame shows the part that will be kept.
*Reset corners* puts the corners back.

=== Masks

*Masks* adjusts part of a photo.
Click *+ Linear gradient* for a graduated mask, such as for a sky, or *+ Radial gradient* for a round one.
Drag the mask's handles on the photo, then set its *Feather*, *Invert*, and its own exposure, contrast, color, clarity, and dehaze.
A checkbox shows the mask's area in red.

=== Undo, history, and comparing

* kbd:[Ctrl+Z] undoes a step, and kbd:[Ctrl+Y] or kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+Z] redoes it.
* *History*, at the end of *Adjust*, lists every step; click one to go back to it.
* *Before* (kbd:[\ ]) shows the original, *Side by side* (kbd:[Y]) shows both, and *Split* (kbd:[Shift+Y]) puts a divider between them.
* The clipping button (kbd:[J]) marks blown highlights in red and crushed shadows in blue.
* *Reset all* in the ··· menu starts over from the original, as one more step you can undo.

=== Copying edits to other photos

*Copy* (kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+C]) copies the photo's settings, and *Paste* (kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+V]) applies them to another photo.
In the library, *Paste settings* on the right-click menu applies them to every selected photo.
The crop and the *Transform* settings are not copied, because they belong to one photo.

=== Exporting

Edits stay inside Picasso until you export.

* In the editor, *Export…* saves the photo you are editing as a new file, `name-edited.jpg` by default.
* For many photos, select them and click *Export…* in the *Photo Tray*, or right-click one.
Choose a *Folder*, a *Format* (JPEG, PNG, or TIFF), a *Size* (*Full size* or a long edge of 4096, 2048, 1600, or 1080 px), and the JPEG *Quality*.

Exported files keep their names with the new extension and are numbered when a name is taken, so nothing is overwritten.
Videos are left out.
The status bar shows the progress with *Stop*, then *Open folder*.

== Maps and places

Maps need an internet connection.
Picasso keeps the map tiles it has shown, so places you have looked at work offline later.

=== Places

image::places.png[Places]

*Places* in the sidebar shows every photo with a location on a map.
Drag to move the map, and zoom with the wheel or a pinch.
Nearby photos join into one pin with a count.
Point at a pin for a card with its photos: click one to open it, or use *Select these photos* or *Zoom in*.
Clicking a pin of several photos zooms in on them, and double-clicking a single photo's pin opens it.
*Open in OpenStreetMap* opens the same view in your web browser.

From a photo, *Show on map* opens Places at that photo.

=== Placing photos by hand

For photos without GPS:

. Select the photos in the library.
. Open *Places*; it keeps the selection.
. Click *Geotag*, then click the map where the photos were taken.

*Clear geotag* removes the pin you placed.
A location from the camera's GPS is never changed.

=== GPS tracks

Picasso reads the `.gpx` and `.kml` files in your folders and draws them as colored paths under the photo pins.
Point at a track for its name, start time, distance, and duration, and double-click it to zoom to it.
*Tracks* in the Places toolbar turns the paths off.

=== Placing photos from a GPS track

If you carried a GPS logger or a phone app while shooting, Picasso can place your photos by matching their times to the track:

. Right-click the folder in the folder tree and choose *Geotag from GPS track…*.
. Choose the *GPS track* file (`.gpx`, `.kml`, or `.kmz`) and check the *Folder*.
. Set the *Camera time zone*: the offset of the camera's clock from UTC, such as `+01:00`.
Cameras usually keep local time without a time zone.
. Click *Geotag*.

Photos that already have GPS from the camera, or a pin you placed, keep their location.
Picasso writes the places to its library and to a `.picasso-geotags.json` file in the folder, never into the photos.

The same works from a terminal, and `--dry-run` shows what would happen without saving anything:

[source,bash]
----
picasso geotag track.gpx ~/Pictures/Trip --offset +01:00 --dry-run
----

== Statistics

image::statistics.png[Statistics]

*Statistics* in the sidebar shows your library in numbers: photos per year and month, the days you took photos, your cameras, lenses, and focal lengths, and how many photos have a location.
Click *All years* or a year to look at one year.
Most items have *Show these photos*, and places have *Show on map*.

== Travel Map

image::travelmap.png[Travel Map]

*Travel Map* colors the countries where your geotagged photos were taken, and the states and provinces of the largest countries.
A deeper color means more photos.

* Switch between *Globe* and *Flat*.
* The side panel counts *Countries visited* and *Continents*, with the *Coverage by continent*.
* Search for a country to find it on the map.
* Click a colored place for a card; *Show photos* shows them in Places.

The Travel Map follows the photos' GPS only, so place more photos to color more of it.
It works offline.

== Finding duplicates

*Duplicates* in the sidebar finds files whose content is identical, byte for byte, even under other names or in other folders.
The search runs in the background and goes on when you close the window; the sidebar shows how many groups it has found.

In each group one file is *Kept*: a starred or edited one when there is one.
Move the other copies to the trash one by one, with *Trash extra copies* for a group, or with *Move all extra copies to trash*.
*Search again* looks once more.

== Files that couldn't be read

When a scan finds files it can't read, such as a damaged photo, a link appears in the status bar.
It opens the list, with *Show in folder* and *Move to trash* for each file.

== Settings

Click the gear at the top right, or press kbd:[Ctrl+,], to open *Settings*.
Settings apply right away.

General::
Language, color theme, thumbnail size, slideshow interval, and whether to ask before moving photos to the trash.
*Updates* can check once a day for a newer version; when there is one, a link to it appears at the top right.
The check only asks GitHub for the latest version number.

Folders::
The folders Picasso watches, each with buttons to open it, rescan it, or remove it from Picasso.
Removing a folder leaves its files on disk.
*Excluded folders* lists subfolders Picasso skips; *Exclude a folder…* adds one and *Include again* brings it back.

File types::
Which files Picasso adds: JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, BMP, TIFF, HEIC/HEIF, camera RAW, and videos.
Files of a type you turn off leave the library but stay on disk.

Map::
The map in Places and the Map panel.
*OpenFreeMap Liberty*, the default, and *OpenStreetMap* need nothing more.
*CARTO* has three styles and needs a free key: *Get a free key* opens CARTO's sign-up page, and you paste the key into the field.
*Esri World Imagery* shows satellite photos.

Storage::
Where the library, the cache, and the settings are kept, how much space each takes, *Clear cache*, and the backups.

=== Folders in the tree

Right-click a folder in the sidebar for:

* *Geotag from GPS track…*
* *Exclude from scanning*, for a subfolder you don't want in the library
* *Remove from Picasso…*, for a folder you added
* *Rescan this folder*

== Your library and its backups

=== What is kept where

The library, `picasso.db`, holds the folders you added, every photo's stars, caption, rotation, place, fixed date, albums, and edits, your presets, and your GPS tracks.
It does not hold the photos themselves, so back up your photos as you would any files.

The cache holds thumbnails and map tiles; clearing it is safe, and Picasso makes them again.
Your choices in Settings are kept in `settings.json`, apart from the library.
*Settings → Storage* shows where each one is.

=== Backups

Picasso backs up the library once a week when it starts, and keeps the five newest.
In *Settings → Storage*, *Back up now* makes one, and *Restore…* puts one back: Picasso backs up the current library first, then restarts with the one you chose.

[#recovery]
=== When the library can't be opened

If the library can't be opened, for example after a disk error, Picasso explains what went wrong instead of starting, and never overwrites the library:

* *Try again*, for when the library was only busy, such as while a backup or sync tool had it open.
* *Restore the backup of …* puts the newest readable backup in its place.
Changes since that backup are lost.
* *Start a new library* starts over with your Pictures folder.
* *Open library folder* shows the files, and *Quit* leaves everything as it is.

Restoring and starting over are offered only when the library file is damaged.
The old file is kept beside the new one, renamed `picasso-damaged-` with the date, so nothing is lost.

=== Moving and renaming folders

Picasso rescans your folders when it starts and watches them while it runs; kbd:[F5] rescans them now.

* Moving photos between folders Picasso already watches needs nothing; the next scan finds them.
* To move a whole folder somewhere new, move it, remove the old place in *Settings → Folders*, and add the new one with *+ Import*.
The photos are recognized and get their stars, captions, albums, and edits back.
* A folder on a drive that isn't plugged in keeps its photos until the drive is back.

A photo that leaves the library keeps its stars, caption, albums, and edits for 90 days, in case it comes back.

== Troubleshooting

Videos don't show or don't play::
Install FFmpeg (see <<ffmpeg>>) and start Picasso again.
Videos found while FFmpeg was missing are added then.

The map is blank::
Maps need an internet connection.
If you picked *CARTO*, check that its key is entered in *Settings → Map*, or switch to *OpenFreeMap Liberty*.

A photo is missing::
Check that its folder is in *Settings → Folders* and not under *Excluded folders*, and that its type is ticked in *Settings → File types*.
Press kbd:[F5] to rescan, and look at the files that couldn't be read in the status bar.

The Mac says Picasso can't be opened::
See <<install>>: use *Open Anyway* in *System Settings → Privacy & Security*.

The AppImage doesn't start::
Run it with `--appimage-extract-and-run` where FUSE is missing, as in containers.

Picasso says the library can't be opened::
See <<recovery>>.
